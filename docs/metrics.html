How long deals last

A deal is the stretch a product spends in stock with at least the minimum discount. Only deals whose start was seen are counted, and deals still running count for as long as they have been watched. A store is read every few hours, so anything shorter than that is missed.

Store-wide price changes

Times a store moved many prices by the same percentage at once while its discounts stayed the same: a repricing or a currency swing, not a sale. Items caught in one are left out of the Price change sort.

    Reading these numbers

    • Typical discount is the middle one: half the store's sale items are marked down more, half less.
    • Still available after N days is the share of deals still in stock at the sale price that long after they began. Half gone within is how long it takes for half to end.
    • A deal ends when the product sells out, when the sale ends (the price goes back up) or when the store takes it down.
    • Cut further counts deals whose price later fell another 5% or more, with the usual wait.
    • Marked-up "was" price counts deals whose crossed-out price is over 5% above every price tracked here before the sale. It can only be checked for products seen before their sale began.
    • A figure based on fewer than deals is shown as "too few".
    • The table at the top counts every deal at every store, so the stores with the most deals weigh most in it.
